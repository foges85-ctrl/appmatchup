What happened to Microsoft Lens

Microsoft Lens has been retired. It was taken off the Apple App Store and Google Play, and it stopped being able to make new scans some weeks later. This page records only what Microsoft itself published about that — the dates, what happens to scans people already have, and where Microsoft directs users now — plus one independent check of whether the App Store listing still exists. It does not attempt to explain the decision, because Microsoft did not. Everything below was read on 2026-08-26.

The three dates Microsoft published

Microsoft’s retirement notice sets out a staged shutdown rather than a single switch-off, and the same schedule applies to iOS and Android.

January 9, 2026

Retirement begins on iOS and Android. Microsoft states the app will no longer be supported after February 9.

February 9, 2026

Microsoft Lens is removed from the Apple App Store and the Google Play Store. From this point it can no longer be installed or reinstalled from either store.

March 9, 2026

The last date on which the app can create scans. Microsoft states that after that, you will not be able to create new scans.

Checked independently on 2026-08-26: the Microsoft Lens listing at apps.apple.com/us/app/microsoft-lens-pdf-scanner/id975925059 returns a 404, and an App Store lookup for track id 975925059 returns no result. There is consequently no App Store link on this page, which is a departure from the other pages on this site.

What happens to scans you already made

Microsoft’s notice separates two things that are easy to run together: the ability to make new scans, which ended on March 9, 2026, and access to scans already made, which did not.

Access to past scans continues if the app is still installed on the device. Microsoft attaches one condition to that: you need to be signed into your last active account on the Microsoft Lens app to reach them. So the practical risk is not the retirement date but the phone. Because the app can no longer be downloaded from either store, deleting it, replacing the device, or setting up a new one without restoring it removes the route back to anything still held inside it.

Microsoft does not say anything about a deadline after which past scans stop being reachable, and it does not describe an export or bulk-download step for getting them out.

Where Microsoft points people now

To one place, and only one. Microsoft’s notice says that to continue using scanning capabilities it recommends the OneDrive app, which includes a built-in scanning feature. It then gives the steps: open OneDrive, use the + button, choose Scan photo, and save the result to OneDrive.

Microsoft also states one limitation of that route on the same page, which is worth carrying over intact because it is the kind of thing that only becomes obvious after the fact: OneDrive does not support saving scans locally on your device. Lens users who kept scans on the phone rather than in a cloud account are being pointed at something that works differently in that specific respect.

That is the whole of Microsoft’s direction, and this page does not extend it. No alternative is recommended here. Readers weighing the wider field of iPhone scanners may find the comparisons on this site useful as a starting point — Adobe Scan vs. CamScanner covers two free scanners from large companies and what each one gates, and SwiftScan vs. Genius Scan covers two independent ones and how differently they publish their prices — but neither is offered as a replacement for Lens, and neither is Microsoft’s suggestion.

Microsoft published no reason

This is stated plainly because the absence is itself the answer to a question people are asking. The retirement notice gives dates, consequences and a recommended alternative. It gives no explanation for the decision — no strategy statement, no reference to another product absorbing the feature, no usage rationale.

Explanations circulate in trade coverage and commentary. None of them is Microsoft, and this page does not repeat them. Anyone who wants the reason on the record does not currently have it from the company that made the decision.

What we couldn’t verify

  • Why Microsoft retired the app, which its own notice does not say
  • Whether scans already saved into OneDrive, Word, PowerPoint or elsewhere are affected in any way, which the notice does not address
  • Whether access to past scans inside the installed app has an end date of its own
  • Whether any export path exists for moving past scans out of the app in bulk
  • Whether the retirement schedule published on this page is the original one or a revised one, since the notice describes the current dates without referring to any previous schedule
  • Whether the Android app followed the identical sequence in practice, beyond Microsoft stating the same dates for both platforms
  • Whether OneDrive’s built-in scanning matches what Lens did feature for feature, which the notice does not claim and does not compare

Where this came from

Every date, consequence and recommendation on this page comes from Microsoft’s own notice, Retirement of Microsoft Lens, read on 2026-08-26. The confirmation that the App Store listing no longer resolves is our own check, made the same day, and is described in section 01. Nothing on this page is drawn from press coverage or third-party commentary. Support pages are revised — check the current notice before relying on any date here.
