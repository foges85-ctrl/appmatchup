APP/MATCHUP

Head-to-head comparisons of mobile apps.

Two apps at a time, compared on the same points, with every claim sourced and dated. Plus what individual apps actually cost, because the App Store will tell you an app is free when it sells a hundred-and-fifty-dollar subscription.

Comparisons are built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, pricing pages and official documentation — with every claim sourced and dated. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed as unverified rather than guessed at.

What makes it different

Two, not twenty

A comparison names two apps and sets them against each other. It is not a ranked list, and it is not a roundup of every app in a category with a paragraph under each.

Where each one fits

Each comparison ends by naming who each app suits, reasoned from the sourced rows above it. Where the answer splits by use case, it says which use case goes which way rather than stopping at “it depends.”

Same page, both sides

Both apps are covered in one document, against the same set of points, so the two columns are actually comparable to each other.

Category hubs

A hub page covers a whole category at once — how the apps in it actually divide, and which comparison to read next.
