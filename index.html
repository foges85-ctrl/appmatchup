APP/MATCHUP

Head-to-head comparisons of mobile apps.

Two apps at a time, compared on the same points, with sources cited — not a directory of listings with a description under each one.

Comparisons are built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, pricing pages and official documentation — with every claim sourced and dated. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed as unverified rather than guessed at.

What makes it different

Two, not twenty

A comparison names two apps and sets them against each other. It is not a ranked list, and it is not a roundup of every app in a category with a paragraph under each.

Where each one fits

Each comparison ends by naming who each app suits, reasoned from the sourced rows above it. Where the answer splits by use case, it says which use case goes which way rather than stopping at “it depends.”

Same page, both sides

Both apps are covered in one document, against the same set of points, so the two columns are actually comparable to each other.

Comparisons

  • Fing vs. Ubiquiti WiFiman — two free iPhone network scanners, compared on pricing, discovery protocols and platform support.
  • Network Analyzer Pro vs. iNet Pro — two paid one-time-purchase iPhone scanners, compared on diagnostic tools, data collection and platform support.
  • Fing vs. Network Analyzer Pro — a subscription scanner against a single-purchase one, compared on pricing model, scan limits and data collection.
  • IP Scanner vs. iNet — two free iPhone scanners with paid unlocks, compared on free-tier limits, remote access and device support.
  • NetScanPro vs. Fing — our own app against a subscription rival, compared on pricing model, scan limits and data collection.
