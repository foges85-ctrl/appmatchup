What Jobber costs

Jobber publishes four named plans with figures against each, which puts it ahead of most of the field-service category. The complication is that the figure a visitor sees is the output of three things at once — which plan, how big they said their team is, and whether a promotion happens to be running — and only one of those is the plan. Everything below is from Jobber’s own pricing page and help centre and from the App Store listing, all read on 2026-08-25.

The four plans

Core

$49/month, or $29/month billed annually. The entry plan.

Connect

$139/month, or $99/month billed annually.

Grow

$199/month, or $149/month billed annually. This is the plan the free trial runs on.

Plus

$499/month, or $399/month billed annually. The top published plan; its help-centre article lists marketing tools, a dedicated support line, custom automations and sales pipeline features above Grow.

Additional users are $29/month each on every plan. There is no free tier — Jobber offers a trial instead.

View on the App Store

The price moves with the dropdown

Jobber’s pricing page carries a team-size selector offering Just me, 2 to 5, 6 to 10, 11 to 15 and 16+. Changing it changes both the number of users each plan includes and the figures shown for the plans.

There is also a billing-period toggle between Monthly and Annual, which is ordinary, and which accounts for the two figures given against each plan above.

The team-size selector is not ordinary, and it is the reason a straight answer to “what does Jobber cost” is harder to give than the four-plan table suggests. Two people can open the same URL, be shown different numbers for the same named plan, and both be looking at Jobber’s real published pricing. Neither is being misled. But a figure quoted from that page without the selector state attached is incomplete, and the figures on this page are the ones the page returns at its default state.

This site has met a pricing page serving more than one set of figures before, and the two cases are worth keeping apart. Where app prices are published records Everlance returning two different price sets in a single fetch, with nothing on the page to say why — which is why no Everlance figure is quoted anywhere here. Jobber’s case is the opposite in one important respect: the control is visible, labelled, and operated by the reader. The figures move because the reader moved them.

That makes Jobber’s page honest and still makes a quoted figure incomplete. A pricing page is not always a price list. Sometimes it is a calculator, and what it returns depends on what it was told.

A promotion is running underneath all of it

At the time of reading, Jobber’s pricing page carries a banner reading “Save up to 40% Offer ends Aug 31”, and the plan figures shown are promotional rather than standard.

The page distinguishes them itself, in the pattern “for 3 months, then” a higher figure on monthly billing, and “for 12 months, then” a higher figure on annual. It states that the offer applies to new customers only and ends August 31 at 11:59 p.m. PT, and it notes that the pricing displayed is annual prepaid with other billing options available.

The figures in the plans section above are the standing ones — the numbers after the word “then” — because those are what the plan costs rather than what it costs this month. That is an editorial choice and worth stating outright: a reader visiting the page before August 31 will see lower numbers than the ones on this page, and both are correct.

Jobber discloses the promotion properly. The reason it belongs on a cost page is timing. Anything quoting Jobber’s pricing during a promotional window captures the promotional rate, and the promotional rate stops being true on a date the promotion names. A figure with an expiry attached needs the expiry carried with it.

The App Store sells a plan Jobber documents nowhere

Jobber’s App Store in-app-purchase list carries six items: Lite Monthly at $29.99, Core Monthly at $69.99, Connect Monthly (1 User) at $139.99, Connect Monthly at $199.99, Grow Monthly (1 User) at $199.99, and Grow Monthly at $399.99.

Set that against what Jobber publishes and three gaps open.

There is no plan called Lite. Jobber’s pricing page names Core, Connect, Grow and Plus. Its help centre has a dedicated article for each of those four and none for a fifth. Lite is sold and not described.

There is no Plus item. Plus is advertised on the pricing page at $499/month and has its own help-centre article setting out what it includes, but nothing of that name appears in the list of what Jobber currently sells through the App Store.

And the Core figure does not match. Jobber’s pricing page gives Core at $49/month. The App Store gives Core Monthly at $69.99. That is a larger gap than a store price point accounts for, and nothing published reconciles the two.

We report the disagreement rather than resolving it. Nothing Jobber publishes says which of these items are current, legacy, promotional or platform-specific, or what Lite is.

How many users a plan includes depends which Jobber page you read

This one matters more than the price disagreements, because on a plan billed per user the included count is half the cost.

Jobber’s pricing page, at its default state, shows Core, Connect and Grow including 1 user each and Plus including 5. Its help-centre article for the Grow plan describes access for up to 10 users. Its help-centre article for the Plus plan describes 15 users.

Those are three different accounts of the same two plans, all published by Jobber, all live on the day of reading. The team-size selector explains part of it — the pricing page’s counts move when the selector does — but the help-centre articles carry no selector and state their figures flatly.

The practical consequence is arithmetic. At $29/month per additional user, the difference between a Grow plan that includes 1 user and one that includes 10 is $261/month, which is more than the plan. A reader working out what Jobber will cost their crew cannot get there from the published material without knowing which count applies to them.

We list this unresolved rather than picking the most favourable reading, or the least.

The trial

Jobber offers 14 days of full access to all its features on the Grow plan, with no credit card required.

Two details are worth separating out. The trial runs on Grow, not on the plan a trialist may intend to buy — so someone evaluating Core is trying a plan above it, and someone evaluating Plus is trying a plan below it. And no credit card is required to start, which distinguishes it from the card-up-front pattern common in this category.

There is no free tier. The trial is the only way to use Jobber without paying, and it ends.

What we couldn’t determine

  • What the Lite plan is, what it includes, or how it relates to Core. It appears only in the App Store in-app-purchase list and is named on no Jobber page consulted
  • How many users each plan includes, for the reason set out above — the pricing page and the two help-centre articles give different counts
  • Whether Plus can be bought from inside the iOS app at all, since no item of that name appears in the in-app-purchase list
  • Why the App Store’s Core Monthly figure of $69.99 differs from the $49/month on Jobber’s pricing page, which nothing published explains
  • What each plan costs at team sizes other than the pricing page’s default, since those figures are only visible by operating the selector
  • Whether Jobber charges transaction fees on payments taken through the app, which neither the App Store listing nor the pricing page states
  • What the standing price will be after the promotion ends, beyond the figures the page currently gives after the word “then”
  • Whether add-on costs sit above the plan price. The Grow help-centre article prices a marketing suite and several individual marketing tools separately, and the pricing page does not fold those into the plan figures

Where this came from and where else Jobber appears

The plan figures, the additional-user rate, the team-size selector, the promotional banner and the trial terms come from Jobber’s pricing page, read on 2026-08-25. The plan user counts and feature descriptions come from Jobber’s help-centre articles on the Plus plan and the Grow plan, read the same day. The in-app-purchase items and the App Store link come from the Jobber App Store listing, also read on 2026-08-25. Prices change and plan contents change with them, and this page was written during a promotional window that the page itself says ends on August 31 — check all four before relying on any figure here.
