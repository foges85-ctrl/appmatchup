What Fing costs

Fing is free to download and the App Store lists it as free, which is where most of the confusion starts. There are three plans, two of them paid, and the feature most people subscribe for needs something running on your network that the phone app can’t provide by itself. Everything below is from Fing’s own pricing page, checked on 2026-08-14.

The three plans

Free

$0. Up to 3 scans per day, internet speed tests, network and ISP reporting, and troubleshooting tools.

Premium

$7.99 a month, or $69.99 a year. Everything in Free, plus continuous monitoring, a full event timeline, blocking and limiting internet access, autoblocking unknown devices, 2 monitored networks, and mobile and email notifications.

Professional

$16.99 a month, or $149.99 a year. Everything in Premium, plus shared workspaces, advanced search across networks, collaboration with multiple users, filter templates and advanced reporting.

Fing’s pricing page states that displayed prices may vary at checkout by region, local taxes or currency. Annual billing is presented on that page as saving up to 30%.

The three-scan cap

The free plan allows up to 3 manual scans per day. That is the number that decides whether the free tier is usable for what you want to do.

In practice, 3 scans is enough to check who is on the network occasionally — a scan now, a scan when a new device shows up, a scan to confirm something left. It is not enough to work through a problem across an afternoon, where the normal pattern is to change one thing, rescan, change the next thing, and rescan again. That loop runs out of scans quickly.

This is the limit people most often hit before subscribing.

Monitoring needs more than the app

Continuous monitoring is the headline Premium feature — the thing that turns Fing from something you open into something that watches the network while you are not looking. It is also the part of the plan with a requirement attached.

Fing’s pricing page states that a monitored network requires an active Fing Desktop or Fing Agent. Fing Desktop runs on a computer. Fing Agent installs on hardware you leave running.

So the subscription alone does not give you monitoring from your phone. It gives you monitoring from a machine that stays on, which you also have to set up and keep powered. The phone app becomes the way you read what that machine sees.

This is disclosed on Fing’s own pricing page, in the same place as the plan features — it is stated, not hidden. But it is easy to miss when you are comparing a monthly price against other scanners, because the other scanner in the comparison usually has no equivalent requirement, and a price-to-price reading does not surface the difference.

Monitored networks

Premium includes 2 monitored networks. Professional includes more, presented on Fing’s page as a scale starting at 3 — the exact count could not be read from the page, and it is listed as unresolved below rather than estimated here.

A monitored network, in Fing’s own terms, is a network kept under continuous control, giving ongoing visibility into devices, changes and security events without manual action. The count matters if you are responsible for more than one location — a home and an office, or several sites — because each one you want watched consumes one of them.

What we couldn’t determine

  • How many monitored networks Professional includes. Fing’s page presents a range beginning at 3, and the figure did not resolve to a single number when the page was read
  • Whether the in-app subscription price on iOS matches the price on Fing’s website, which App Store listings do not show
  • What “up to 30%” saving on annual billing applies to, since the published monthly and annual figures imply different discounts on each plan
  • Whether the 3-scan daily cap counts automatic scans or only manual ones, which the page does not specify
