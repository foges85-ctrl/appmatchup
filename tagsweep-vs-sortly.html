TagSweep vs. Sortly

TagSweep and Sortly both scan barcodes into an inventory you can export, and both are free up to a hundred records. They are not the same kind of tool: Sortly is a cloud inventory platform for tracking what a business owns over time, and TagSweep is a scanner that produces a spreadsheet and hands it to whatever system you already use. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes, with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such. TagSweep is published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which also operates AppMatchup; see the disclosure below.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-21 — with Sortly’s pricing page re-checked on 2026-08-25 — and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

TagSweep

A scanner for asset tags that reads the barcode and the printed number together and exports a spreadsheet, built for school device inventories.

Published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which operates AppMatchup.

View on the App Store

Sortly

A cloud inventory platform organised into folders and items, with barcode scanning, label generation and tiers priced per user licence.

View on the App Store

Sortly is where an inventory lives. TagSweep is how a list of devices gets collected and handed over — it produces a file for the system your district already made you use, and then it is done. If you need somewhere to keep the inventory, Sortly is that. If you already have somewhere and the hard part is the hallway, they are solving different problems.

Where each one fits

TagSweep, which we publish, costs $29.99 once rather than a recurring subscription, includes every feature on its free tier rather than gating export behind a paid plan, reads the barcode and the printed number together so a worn label still resolves, hands devices out against an imported roster, and keeps everything on the device with nothing uploaded. The limits are plain and worth stating outright. It is a single-device tool with no team access and no cloud. It does not store or manage an inventory over time. It offers no alerts, no pick lists and no reporting. And it is two weeks old with one App Store rating, against Sortly’s fourteen years — there is no track record here to judge it by.

Sortly has fourteen years on the App Store, gives the inventory somewhere to actually live, syncs across a team, generates labels, and offers alerts and reporting, with folders and photos for items you handle physically. Against that, it is a recurring cost that rises with users and items, it requires iOS 18.5 where TagSweep runs on 17.0, its free plan allows one user licence, and its yearly rate is stated on its own page as a first-year discount.
