Referra vs. Contacts Journal CRM

Referra and Contacts Journal CRM are both free to download, and both ask you to log your own interactions rather than reading them from your inbox. They differ in scope: Contacts Journal is a general relationship CRM for anyone with clients, and Referra tracks one thing — who refers business to a cash-pay or private-pay practice. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes, with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such. Referra is published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which also operates AppMatchup; see the disclosure below.

These differ in scope more than in kind. Contacts Journal is a general relationship CRM that any client-facing professional can bend to their own workflow. Referra tracks one workflow — who refers business to a practice — and does not try to be anything else. If your work is not referral-driven, the narrower tool has nothing extra to offer you.

Where each one fits

Referra, which we publish, keeps analytics, interactions and reminders on its free tier rather than gating them, offers a $79.99 one-time purchase alongside the subscription, reports referral-specific analytics, and holds data in the user’s own iCloud rather than the developer’s. The limits are plain. It covers one workflow. It offers no company accounts, no custom fields, no duplicate detection and no contact mapping. Its listing does not mention importing contacts. And it is a far newer and far less used app — the rating counts in the table above show the gap.

Contacts Journal CRM has seventeen years on the App Store and a much broader feature set, with tags, groups, filters, company accounts, custom fields, duplicate detection and merge, contact mapping and CSV import, used across many client-facing professions. Against that, its developer publishes no price for the in-app upgrade, and it offers no referral tracking or analytics of any kind.
