Cloze vs. Contacts Journal CRM

Cloze is published by Cloze, Inc. and has been on the App Store since 2013-02-19. Contacts Journal CRM is published by zaal LLC and has been available since 2009-09-14. Both are free to download with paid upgrades, and both are relationship CRMs rather than sales pipelines. What separates them is where the record comes from — Cloze pulls it from your email, calls and meetings, and Contacts Journal is what you write down yourself. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and Cloze’s own pricing page — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-12 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Cloze

A relationship CRM that builds its own record by pulling from your email, calls, meetings and messages, with AI prompts and team features, sold by subscription.

View on the App Store

Contacts Journal CRM

A manual relationship log — you write the notes, set the follow-ups and attach the files — with tags, custom fields and company accounts, sold as an in-app upgrade.

View on the App Store

The question is whether you want the record kept for you or kept by you. Cloze watches your email and calls and assembles the history automatically, which means giving it that access and paying a monthly fee. Contacts Journal asks you to log what happened, which is more work and less to hand over.

Where each one fits

Cloze is the automatic option, assembling a history from email, calls and meetings without data entry, and it adds email tracking, team features and 4 languages on top. Against that, it costs $17 to $19.99 a month for one person before any team plan, and building that record means connecting your email and messages to it.

Contacts Journal CRM is the one where everything is entered by you, which means nothing is connected and nothing is watched, with tags, custom fields, company accounts and contact mapping on top. Against that, its developer publishes no price for the in-app upgrade, it offers no team features, and it is English only with a higher iOS floor at 17.0 against Cloze’s 16.0.
