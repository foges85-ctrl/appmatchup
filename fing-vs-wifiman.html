Fing vs. Ubiquiti WiFiman

Fing is published by Fing Limited and has been on the App Store since 2011-04-19. Ubiquiti WiFiman is published by Ubiquiti Inc. and has been available since 2018-08-04. Both are free to download and both run on iPhone and iPad. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, Fing’s pricing page and Ubiquiti’s help centre — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-08 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Fing

A general-purpose network scanner with a free tier and paid subscription plans, sold independently of any hardware.

View on the App Store

Ubiquiti WiFiman

A free companion app for Ubiquiti UniFi networks, with scanning and speed-testing alongside its UniFi-specific tools.

View on the App Store

If you already run UniFi hardware, WiFiman is free and covers the ground you need. If you don’t, its distinguishing features don’t apply to you, and Fing works with any network — though its free tier is capped.

Where each one fits

Ubiquiti WiFiman is free, and no scan limit is stated for it. The features that set it apart are built around Ubiquiti hardware — UniFi device discovery, Teleport VPN into a UniFi network, and a Floorplan Mapper on LiDAR-equipped devices — so those parts apply only to someone already running UniFi gear. Its desktop app supports only UniFi device discovery and Teleport VPN, which points the same way. It offers no continuous monitoring or alerts.

Fing covers more ground on paper, and none of it is tied to one vendor’s hardware — it lists port scanning, speed tests, continuous monitoring, and Windows, Mac, Raspberry Pi and web clients alongside the phone app. Two limits sit against that. Its free plan allows up to 3 manual scans per day, with unlimited scans on paid plans at $7.99/month or $69.99/year for Premium and $16.99/month or $149.99/year for Professional. And its continuous monitoring and alerts require Fing Desktop, Fing Agent or Fingbox running separately, so the phone app on its own does not provide them. What Fing costs sets out all three plans and what the monitoring tier needs beyond the app.

What we couldn’t verify

  • Whether Fing’s free tier can scan without creating a Fing account
  • How accurately either app identifies devices in practice, which no published source establishes
  • What Fing’s “patented” device-recognition technology covers, which its App Store description asserts without detail
  • Whether Fing’s iOS app displays advertising — WiFiman’s listing states it is ad-free, and Fing’s says nothing either way
