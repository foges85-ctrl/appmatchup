SwiftScan vs. Genius Scan

SwiftScan is published by Maple Media Apps, LLC and has been on the App Store since 2014-04-03. Genius Scan is published by The Grizzly Labs and has been available since 2010-06-22. Both are free to download, both run on iPhone and iPad, and both charge for roughly the same category of extras — text recognition, cloud export, sync and stronger document security. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, SwiftScan’s features page and The Grizzly Labs’ pricing page — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-26 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

SwiftScan

A general document scanner with OCR, cloud export and built-in fax, sold as subscription tiers whose names appear nowhere until the purchase sheet opens.

View on the App Store

Genius Scan

A document scanner whose free version is presented as complete in itself, with a single annual subscription for text recognition, cloud export and sync.

View on the App Store

Two free downloads that charge for the same kinds of extras. The difference is that one of them tells you what those extras are and what they cost before you install, and the other tells you nothing until you are already inside the app.

Where each one fits

Genius Scan suits someone who wants to know what they are agreeing to before they agree to it. The pricing page names three plans, attaches a figure to each, and says what each one adds, and the App Store description marks the premium features inline so the free boundary can be read off the feature list itself. The free version is presented as a complete scanner rather than a trial: detection, correction, filters, batch scanning, merging and splitting, multi-page PDFs, tagging and search are all unmarked, and email is an unmarked export route. What Ultra adds at $39.99/year is text recognition, the named cloud destinations, backup and sync, and the security features. The clarity is not total — the App Store description sells a “Genius Scan Plus plan” and a separate Genius Cloud subscription that the pricing page never mentions, and the purchase list carries Ultra at three different annual-looking figures — but a reader can still arrive at a number before installing anything.

SwiftScan suits someone who wants a scanner with fax built in and is willing to find out the price at the purchase sheet. It covers more ground than Genius Scan on paper, and it covers it without tier markings: OCR, redaction, e-signatures, password protection, a long list of cloud destinations and fax all appear in one undifferentiated feature list, so nothing published indicates which of them survive without paying. Sending a fax is a separate purchase from any subscription. It also asks less of the device than Genius Scan does — the two listings set different minimum iOS floors, which the At a glance table above records. The practical consequence is that the download decision and the pricing decision cannot be made at the same time. What SwiftScan costs sets out the full purchase list, the second listing that sells for $199.99, and the third-party lifetime offer that has carried a $199.99 regular price for the past year.

If what you actually want is a scanner from a company large enough that the pricing question turns into a different question — which account you need and which subscription the extras belong to — Adobe Scan vs. CamScanner covers that pair.

What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • What SwiftScan does without a subscription, since no source distinguishes its free features from its paid ones
  • What SwiftScan’s Plus, VIP and Pro tiers each unlock, and why Plus Monthly is listed twice at two prices
  • Whether Genius Scan’s “Genius Scan Plus plan” is the Plus item in the purchase list, a former name for Ultra, or a third thing — the pricing page does not use the name
  • What Genius Cloud costs, or whether it is still sold separately, since no item of that name appears in the purchase list while the App Store description calls it a separate subscription
  • Why Genius Scan Ultra appears at three different figures in the purchase list, which nothing published explains
  • Whether either app requires an account to scan and export locally, which neither developer states
  • How either app’s scan quality or text recognition performs in practice, which no published source establishes
