Network Analyzer Pro vs. iNet Pro

Network Analyzer Pro is published by Jiri Techet and has been on the App Store since 2012-09-18. iNet Pro is published by BananaGlue GmbH and has been available since 2009-04-10. Both are paid one-time purchases with no subscription. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and both developers’ own privacy policies — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-08 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Network Analyzer Pro

A broad network diagnostics toolkit — LAN scanning alongside ping, traceroute, DNS lookup, whois and speed testing — from an independent developer in Czechia.

iNet Pro

A network scanner built around device inventory and remote control of Macs, from a German studio, sold as a one-time purchase.

If you want one app that also handles traceroute, DNS lookups and speed tests, Network Analyzer Pro is the broader toolkit and the cheaper one. If what you want is a labelled inventory you can act on — waking Macs, opening SSH or VNC sessions, exporting the list — iNet Pro is built around that, and its developer states it collects no data at all.

Where each one fits

Network Analyzer Pro is the broader diagnostic toolkit of the two, at $3.99, and it lists tools iNet Pro does not — traceroute with map view and AS number data, whois, DNS lookup including DNSSEC record types, and an internet speed test. Two things sit against that. It requires iOS 18.0, which excludes older devices iNet Pro still supports at 15.0. And its developer’s own privacy policy discloses Google Firebase Crashlytics and Analytics, with no in-app option to turn them off.

iNet Pro costs $11.99 and is the narrower of the two on diagnostics — network scanning, a Bonjour service monitor and an Apple AirPort monitor. It is the only one of the two that lists remote access and control: SSH, VNC and HTTP connections, remote sleep, shutdown and restart of Macs, and SSH commands. It is also the only one listing export of scan results, to CSV or PDF. Its developer states it collects no data.

What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • What iNet Pro’s free version charges for its in-app upgrades, and whether they total more or less than the $11.99 paid app
  • Whether Network Analyzer Pro’s Firebase analytics can be disabled from inside the app — its policy states there is no in-app toggle, but does not say whether one is planned
  • Whether iNet Pro’s AirPort monitor remains useful, given the developer’s own note that the newest AirPort models are unsupported because Apple removed SNMP
  • Whether BananaGlue’s privacy statement covers third-party crash reporting; it addresses data sent to BananaGlue and does not mention analytics SDKs either way
  • How either app performs in practice, which no published source establishes
