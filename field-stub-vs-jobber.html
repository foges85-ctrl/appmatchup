Field Stub vs. Jobber

Field Stub and Jobber are both free to download, and both are used by people who bill for work done at a client’s site. They are not the same kind of tool: Field Stub is a single-person visit logbook sold as a one-time unlock, and Jobber is a field service platform for a team, sold per user by subscription. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — Jobber’s App Store listing and pricing page, and Field Stub’s product page — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such. Field Stub is published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which also operates AppMatchup; see the disclosure below.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-10 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Field Stub

A visit logbook for a solo IT contractor, storing everything on the device and sold as a one-time unlock.

Published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which operates AppMatchup.

Jobber

A field service platform covering quoting, scheduling, dispatch, invoicing and payments across a team, priced per user.

These are not competing for the same buyer. Jobber runs a business — scheduling a crew, quoting work, sending invoices, taking payment. Field Stub documents one person’s visits and produces a record to hand a client. If you have people to dispatch and invoices to chase, Jobber is the category. If you are the whole company and you need the visit written down properly, Field Stub is far narrower and costs once.

Where each one fits

Field Stub is $39.99 once rather than a recurring per-user subscription, its free tier is a genuine free tier rather than a trial, everything is stored on the device with no account, and it works fully offline. The limits are plain. It does no scheduling, no dispatch, no quoting and no invoicing. It supports one user only. Its free tier stops at five clients. And it is a far newer and far less used app — the rating counts in the table above show the gap.

Jobber is the broader tool by a wide margin, covering quoting, scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, payments and QuickBooks integration across a team, with a decade on the App Store behind it. Against that, it is a recurring cost that rises with every user added, it offers a trial rather than a free tier, and it is built for businesses with staff rather than for one person documenting their own visits.
