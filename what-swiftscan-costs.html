What SwiftScan costs

SwiftScan is free to download. Beyond that, the developer publishes nothing: no pricing page, no tier names, no figures, not a sentence about what the free app keeps and what it withholds. Three tier names — Plus, VIP and Pro — exist only inside the App Store’s in-app-purchase list, where one of them is listed twice at two different prices. Meanwhile a separate App Store listing under the same name sells for $199.99 up front, and a third-party deal listing has been marking a “lifetime” plan down from $199.99 for at least a year. Everything below was read on 2026-08-26.

Which SwiftScan this is

Three listings on the US App Store carry the SwiftScan name, and they are not variations on one product. Before any price figure means anything, it has to be attached to the right one.

SwiftScan AI Document Scanner

Track id 834854351. Free to download, published by Maple Media Apps, LLC, and on the App Store since 2014-04-03. This is the listing this page covers, and it is the one the developer’s own site links to for iOS.

SwiftScan Pro Document Scanner

Track id 1007355333. $199.99 to download, published by Maple Media Apps, LLC, and on the App Store since 2015-07-15. Its own description opens by saying it “unlocks full access to all the features limited in SwiftScan’s app”. Its in-app purchases are fax credits and nothing else, at $1.99, $14.99, $19.99, $49.99 and $69.99.

SwiftScan: PDF Scanner & Tools

Track id 6794253981. Free to download, published by Disha Thummar, and on the App Store since 2026-07-30. A different developer and a different product that happens to share the name. It is listed here so it can be told apart, and it is not discussed further.

Reading a price for one of these onto another is the easy mistake to make. The $199.99 belongs to the second listing. The first one, which is the one most people install, costs nothing to download.

View on the App Store

The developer publishes no prices at all

swiftscan.app has a features page and a support link. It has no pricing page — the obvious address for one returns a 404 — and no plans page under any other name. The features page lists what the app does at length and marks nothing on that list as paid, so a reader cannot tell from it which capabilities require money.

The App Store description does the same. It runs through scanning, OCR, fax, cloud export, annotation, redaction, e-signatures, encryption and themes without once naming a tier or attaching a figure to anything. The only reference to money in the whole listing is Apple’s standard subscription boilerplate about renewal and cancellation, which every subscription app carries and which names no amount.

This is a stronger version of a pattern this site keeps finding. Some developers bury their prices on a help page or split them across product pages. Here there is no page to bury them on. Nothing the developer publishes, in any medium, tells a prospective user what SwiftScan costs or what the free version stops doing.

What the in-app-purchase list shows

That leaves one source, and it is generated by Apple from what the developer currently sells rather than written by anyone. The free listing’s in-app-purchase list carries ten items:

  • SwiftScan VIP Monthly — $5.99
  • SwiftScan Pro-Full Scanner App — $6.99
  • SwiftScan Plus Monthly — $7.99
  • Credits to Fax from iPhone — $1.99
  • SwiftScan Plus Annual — $59.99
  • SwiftScan VIP Annual — $34.99
  • SwiftScan VIP-Full Scanner App — $8.99
  • SwiftScan Pro 40 — $3.99
  • SwiftScan Plus Monthly — $9.99
  • SwiftScan Plus Annual — $59.99

Four things are worth pulling out of that list, and all four are things the developer’s own material would normally settle.

SwiftScan Plus Monthly appears twice, at $7.99 and at $9.99. Nothing published says which a new subscriber is offered, or what distinguishes the two. SwiftScan Plus Annual also appears twice, at $59.99 both times.

On these figures the tiers do not rank the way their names suggest. VIP is the cheaper of the two subscription tiers — $5.99 a month against $7.99 or $9.99, and $34.99 a year against $59.99. A reader who assumed VIP sat above Plus would have it backwards, and no published document says otherwise either way.

Three items are named nowhere else in any source: SwiftScan Pro 40 at $3.99, SwiftScan Pro-Full Scanner App at $6.99, and SwiftScan VIP-Full Scanner App at $8.99. What they include, and whether they are one-time unlocks or subscriptions, cannot be established from anything the developer publishes.

Fax is billed separately from all of it. Credits to Fax from iPhone is its own $1.99 item on the free listing, and the $199.99 listing sells nothing but fax credits, which means paying for a tier and paying to send a fax are two different transactions.

The $199.99 refers to two different things

The first is straightforward. SwiftScan Pro Document Scanner, the second listing described above, costs $199.99 to download from the App Store. That is a real, chargeable price for a real listing, and anyone who buys that listing pays it.

The second is not the same figure attached to the same thing. A deal listing titled “SwiftScan VIP: Lifetime Subscription” on StackSocial shows $199.99 struck through as the regular price, a sale price of $59.99, and a 70% off badge, read on 2026-08-26. That $199.99 is presented as the regular price of a lifetime of the VIP tier — the tier the App Store sells at $5.99 a month or $34.99 a year.

What we can establish about that regular price is limited, and worth stating precisely. It does not appear on the developer’s site, which publishes no figures. It does not appear in the App Store’s in-app-purchase list, which carries no $199.99 item and no lifetime item of any kind. And every dated observation of the offer we could find over the past twelve months shows it discounted rather than sold at that price:

  • 2025-08-29 — $41.99 with the code TAKE30, described as $158 off, in a Popular Science piece produced as sponsored content by Stack Commerce
  • 2025-11-16 — $59.99, in a PCWorld piece labelled a Sponsored DealPost, which states that the publication may earn a commission on purchases made through links in its articles
  • 2026-05-18 — $59.99, in a Boing Boing piece that discloses it earns a commission on purchases made through links in the post
  • 2026-08-26 — $59.99 on the StackSocial listing itself, read today

Those four are cited as evidence of what the offer displayed on those dates and for nothing else. Every one of them carries an affiliate-commission or sponsored-content disclosure, which means none of them is neutral reporting about the product, and they are named here rather than linked for that reason.

We are not drawing a conclusion from this, and nothing improper is alleged. Struck-through regular prices are ordinary retail practice, promotional windows can run back to back for legitimate reasons, and a lifetime plan sold through a reseller need not have any relationship to a subscription price on Apple’s store. What a reader can take from it is narrow and factual: the only figures traceable to the App Store for the VIP tier are $5.99 a month and $34.99 a year, the $199.99 attached to VIP appears only on third-party listings alongside a discount, and the one place $199.99 is genuinely charged is a different listing selling a different product.

What we couldn’t determine

  • What the free version of SwiftScan does and stops doing, which nothing the developer publishes states
  • What Plus, VIP and Pro each unlock, and how they differ from one another
  • Why SwiftScan Plus Monthly is listed twice at $7.99 and $9.99, and which figure a new subscriber sees
  • What SwiftScan Pro 40, SwiftScan Pro-Full Scanner App and SwiftScan VIP-Full Scanner App are, or whether they are one-time purchases
  • Whether the lifetime VIP plan sold through third parties is the same tier as the VIP items on the App Store, since only the name connects them
  • Whether $199.99 has ever been charged for a VIP subscription, as opposed to appearing as a regular price beside a discount
  • Whether the free listing and the $199.99 listing are the same app behind the paywall, which the descriptions imply but do not state
  • Who acquired the app. Scanbot SDK’s own post of 2022-09-01 says the company was founded as doo GmbH in 2011 and that it sold its consumer app Scanbot when it moved from B2C to B2B. The App Store now shows Maple Media Apps, LLC as the seller of both SwiftScan listings, and the copyright line on both reads © Maple Media LLC. Nothing published connects those two facts to each other, so they are left side by side here rather than joined up
