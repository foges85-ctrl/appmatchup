Sortly vs. Orca Scan

Sortly is published by Sortly Inc. and has been on the App Store since 2012-06-05. Orca Scan is published by Orca Scan Limited and has been available since 2016-11-03. Both scan barcodes into an inventory you can export, and both start free with a hundred-record ceiling. What separates them is what happens when you outgrow that — Sortly moves you to a bigger plan, Orca Scan sells you more of what you ran out of. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and both companies’ own pricing pages — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-21 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Sortly

A mobile inventory platform organised into folders and items, with barcode scanning, label generation and tiered subscriptions.

View on the App Store

Orca Scan

A barcode scanner that writes into spreadsheet-like sheets you design yourself, with per-unit pricing and an API.

View on the App Store

Both free plans stop at the same size, and the bill after that is shaped differently. Sortly’s plans bundle items, users and jobs together, so growing on one axis moves you up a whole tier. Orca Scan charges separately for extra rows, sheets and users, so you pay for the thing you actually ran out of. Which is cheaper depends entirely on which limit you hit first.

Where each one fits

Sortly brings fourteen years on the App Store, folders and photos for items you handle physically, label generation included on the paid tiers, and a lower effective yearly rate at $24 a month in the first year. Against that, it requires iOS 18.5, which excludes a large number of working devices, its free plan allows one user, and the advertised yearly saving halves after the first year. Sortly vs. BoxHero sets the same tier against one carrying twice the items at the same annual price.

Orca Scan sets its floor at iOS 12.0, which runs on almost anything, prices per unit so a single tight limit doesn’t force a whole tier upgrade, exports directly to Excel and Google Sheets, and connects to databases and an API. Against that, its free plan allows one sheet of 100 rows, its revision history is capped by tier, and paying per row, sheet and user makes the eventual bill harder to predict.

What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • What Sortly’s tier above Ultra costs or includes. Its pricing page shows further figures that did not resolve into a named plan when the page was read
  • Whether Sortly’s free plan has a revision history at all, which its pricing page does not state
  • What Orca Scan’s Enterprise plan includes beyond custom limits, since its page lists the tier as contact-for-pricing alongside a figure
  • Whether either app’s in-app subscription price matches the price on its website, which App Store listings do not show
  • Whether Sortly’s 100-item free limit counts quantities or only distinct items — its page says quantities of a unique item do not count, but does not define the boundary further
