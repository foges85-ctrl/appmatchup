Dex vs. Mesh

Dex is published by Dana HQ, Inc. and has been on the App Store since 2021-03-30. Mesh is published by Clay Software, Inc. and has been available since 2019-11-07 — its listing notes it was formerly called Clay. Both are personal CRMs that build a picture of your contacts from your email, calendar and social accounts. They differ most on how you start: Dex has no free tier, and Mesh’s free tier asks for a card. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and both companies’ own pricing pages — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-12 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Dex

A personal CRM built around keep-in-touch reminders and LinkedIn sync, sold entirely by subscription with no free tier.

View on the App Store

Mesh

A contacts app and lightweight CRM that assembles contact cards automatically from your accounts, free up to a contact limit.

View on the App Store

Mesh lets you keep using it without paying, as long as your address book stays under its limit — though it wants a card on file to start. Dex does not have a free tier at all; after the trial you subscribe or you stop. If you want to try a personal CRM without committing, that difference is the whole decision.

Where each one fits

Dex has the deeper LinkedIn integration, at 2,500 connections on Premium and 9,000 on Professional, and it adds a browser extension covering several social networks, custom fields, a map view, and clients on mobile, web, desktop and browser. Against that, there is no free tier at all, it starts at $12 a month even billed annually, and it requires iOS 18.0 where Mesh runs on 17.0.

Mesh has a genuinely free plan with the core features and a 1,000-contact ceiling, assembles contact cards automatically from six sources, and charges $10 a month on Pro if you outgrow it. Against that, the free plan asks for a credit card up front, and its listing does not say which platforms it runs on beyond iOS. A 1,000-contact free tier without the card requirement is what Contacts+ offers in Contacts+ vs. CircleBack.

What we couldn’t verify

  • What Mesh charges a free user who exceeds 1,000 contacts, which its pricing page does not describe
  • Whether Mesh’s free plan charges the card on file at any point, or only holds it
  • Whether Dex’s browser extension is required for the social sources it lists, or optional
  • How completely either app’s automatic contact enrichment works in practice, which no published source establishes
