Jobber vs. Housecall Pro

Jobber is published by Octopusapp Inc and Housecall Pro by Codefied Inc. Both are field service platforms for home-service businesses — scheduling, dispatch, quotes, invoicing and payments — and both are free to download with the platform sold by subscription. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and both companies’ own pricing pages — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-10 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Jobber

A field service platform for quoting, scheduling, invoicing and payments, aimed at trades from HVAC to landscaping, priced per user.

Housecall Pro

A home-services platform with heavy emphasis on customer communication and online booking, sold in tiers by team size.

Jobber has the cheaper entry point and prices by the user, which suits a small crew that wants to add seats as it grows. Housecall Pro costs more to start but leads on the customer-facing side — online booking, on-my-way texts, review requests — which matters more if winning and keeping residential customers is the constraint rather than managing the work.

Where each one fits

Jobber is half the entry price at $29/month, and it prices per user, so the cost scales predictably as a crew grows rather than stepping up in tiers. It also integrates with QuickBooks. Against that, its listing does not mention online booking, and its customer-communication features are described far more thinly than Housecall Pro’s. Field Stub vs. Jobber sets the same per-user subscription against a one-time purchase. Field Stub is published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which operates this site.

Housecall Pro has the stronger customer-facing side — online booking through the business’s website, Yelp or Facebook, automated reminders and confirmations, on-my-way texts and review requests — and it offers a 14-day trial. Against that, it starts at $59 to $79 a month depending on how you pay, its higher tiers are quoted by sales rather than published, and its listing does not mention accounting integration.
