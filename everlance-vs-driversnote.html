Everlance vs. Driversnote

Everlance is published by Everlance Inc. and Driversnote by Driversnote ApS. Both track mileage automatically and both offer a free tier with a cap. What separates them is scope — Driversnote does mileage, Everlance adds expenses, deductions and tax filing — and how plainly each publishes its prices. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes on its own pricing and feature pages, with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-10 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Everlance

A broad self-employed finance app — mileage, expenses, deduction finding and tax filing — sold on tiered subscriptions.

Driversnote

A focused automatic mileage tracker with a published two-tier price, available on phone and web.

Driversnote does mileage and publishes exactly what it costs. Everlance does considerably more — expenses, deductions, tax filing — but its own pricing page showed us two different prices in the same visit, so you should confirm what you’d actually pay before subscribing. If you want one number for one job, Driversnote is the simpler proposition.

Where each one fits

Everlance is far the broader of the two, covering expenses, deductions, tax filing and audit defence alongside mileage, and its free tier is the more generous on trips — 30 automatically detected trips a month against Driversnote’s 15. Against that, its own pricing page showed conflicting figures when we read it, and its free tier is a 7-day trial away from tiers whose cost we could not confirm.

Driversnote does one job and publishes one price for it, Pro at 11 USD a month excluding taxes, and it is available on iOS, Android and web, with an editable log and a public API. Against that, its free tier reports only 15 trips, and it does nothing beyond mileage.

What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • What Everlance actually charges. Its pricing page returned two different figure sets in one visit, most likely a live pricing test
  • Whether Everlance’s free Basic tier requires a trial to start, since the page states every plan includes a 7-day trial
  • Whether Driversnote limits trips recorded on the free tier or only trips reported, which its pricing page does not distinguish
  • What each app’s iOS listing states, since Apple’s lookup service was unavailable when this page was written
  • How accurately either app’s automatic detection captures drives in practice, which no published source establishes
