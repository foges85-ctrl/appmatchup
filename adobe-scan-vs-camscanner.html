Adobe Scan vs. CamScanner

Adobe Scan is published by Adobe Inc. and has been on the App Store since 2017-05-31. CamScanner is published by INTSIG Information Co., Ltd and has been available since 2010-08-28. Both are free to download, both come from companies large enough to run a real support site, and neither one publishes a price on the page a scanner user would think to check. The question that separates them is not what they cost but what shape the cost takes — which account you need, which subscription the extras belong to, and what each free tier is documented to withhold. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes, with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-26 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Adobe Scan

A free scanner that feeds Adobe’s PDF ecosystem, where the paid capabilities belong to an Acrobat subscription rather than to the scanner itself.

CamScanner

A long-established free scanner with its own membership tiers, published on the developer’s site as detailed feature lists with no figure attached to any of them.

View on the App Store

Neither company answers the price question where a scanner user would ask it. What separates them is where the answer lives instead — for one it sits on a different product’s pricing page, and for the other it appears only on the purchase sheet.

Where each one fits

Adobe Scan fits someone who is already inside Adobe’s PDF ecosystem, or expects to be. It needs an Adobe account, and it puts scans into Adobe Document Cloud by default, which is the same storage the free tier gives 5 GB of. The free app is a capable scanner with one clearly published limit — text recognition stops at 5 pages — and the things it will not do are the things a PDF suite does: combining files, converting to other formats, password protection, editing text in a scan. That is the part worth understanding before installing, because those capabilities are not sold as a scanner upgrade. Adobe’s own FAQ says they arrive by subscribing to Adobe Acrobat, Adobe Export PDF, Adobe Acrobat PDF Pack or Adobe Acrobat Pro, and the figures for those live on the Acrobat pricing page, not on any Adobe Scan page: Acrobat Standard at US$14.99/mo and Acrobat Pro at US$19.99/mo on an annual plan billed monthly, US$239.88/yr prepaid for Pro, or US$29.99/mo for Pro month-to-month, with Acrobat Studio above them at US$24.99/mo, US$299.88/yr or US$34.99/mo. Someone who installs the scanner expecting a scanner-sized subscription is looking at a PDF-suite-sized one, and nothing in the app’s own marketing surfaces that.

CamScanner fits someone who wants the scanner and its extras to be one product rather than a doorway into another. Its membership is its own, and INTSIG documents what that membership contains in unusual detail — editable OCR output, ten gigabytes of added cloud space, forty extra collaborators, expiring encrypted links, automatic upload to six named services, translation and cloud OCR with monthly credit allowances, a thirty-day recycle bin. The gap is at the other end. None of it carries a price, on INTSIG’s site or on camscanner.com, and the App Store purchase list that would settle the question is inconsistent with itself: two different product names for what sounds like one membership, the same durations listed twice under differing punctuation, a description promising weekly and quarterly billing that the purchase list does not offer, and a Business Version documented on the developer’s site with no corresponding item to buy. Fax is separate again, priced per page.

Between them, the free tiers are the clearer half of the picture. Adobe publishes a hard number for its free limit and CamScanner publishes a storage figure and a collaborator cap, which is more than either company publishes about what the paid tiers cost. For a pair where one developer publishes a plan table with real figures and the other publishes nothing at all, SwiftScan vs. Genius Scan covers the same category from the opposite end. What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • What a CamScanner Premium Account costs, since neither INTSIG’s site nor camscanner.com publishes a figure for any tier
  • Whether CamScanner’s “Premium Account” and “CamScanner Premium” purchase items are the same membership under two names, and what the third and fourth figures cover
  • What the CamScanner Business Version costs or how it is bought, given that no item matching it appears in the purchase list
  • Whether CamScanner requires an account to scan and store documents on the device alone
  • Whether CamScanner applies a watermark to scans made without a membership. INTSIG lists adding annotations and watermarks as something a Standard account can do, and publishes nothing about a watermark being applied automatically either way
  • Which Adobe Scan premium features come with which of the four subscriptions Adobe names, since the FAQ lists the subscriptions together without dividing the features between them
  • What the Adobe Scan Premium and Adobe Scan Plus purchase items cost relative to an Acrobat plan, and whether buying one inside the app is equivalent to subscribing on Adobe’s site
  • Why Adobe Scan Premium appears at five different figures in the purchase list, which nothing published explains
  • How either app’s scan quality or text recognition performs in practice, which no published source establishes
