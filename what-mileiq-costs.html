What MileIQ costs

MileIQ is free to download and free to use up to a point. There are two plans for individuals, and the difference between them is a monthly cap on how many drives the app will record. Everything below is from MileIQ’s own pricing page, read on 2026-08-14.

The two plans

Free

$0, with 40 free drives every month.

Unlimited

$11.66 a month billed annually, or $13.99 a month billed monthly. Unlimited drives.

MileIQ also sells a separate Teams product for tracking employee mileage, priced separately. This page does not cover it.

View on the App Store

What 40 drives a month means

A drive is a single trip the app records. That makes the cap a count of journeys, not of miles and not of days — a short hop across town and a long haul between cities each spend one of the 40, and a day with several stops spends several.

The practical shape follows from that. Someone driving to a handful of appointments a week may stay under the cap without thinking about it. Anyone driving between jobs daily, with each leg recorded separately, will not.

The cap is the only limit MileIQ names on the free plan.

What the pricing page doesn’t settle

MileIQ’s pricing page renders the same three-item feature list beneath both plans: automatic mileage tracking, auto-generated reports, and unlimited drives.

That third item is the problem, because the free plan is simultaneously described as 40 drives a month. The two statements sit on the same page about the same plan, so the list as rendered does not establish whether anything besides the cap differs between Free and Unlimited.

We report this rather than resolve it. Reading the feature list literally would mean the free plan includes unlimited drives, which the plan’s own description contradicts. The safest reading is the one the plan names give: the paid plan removes the cap. That is what this site goes with, and it is an interpretation rather than something the page states outright.

Who publishes it

The App Store lists MileIQ Inc. as the seller.

The footer of mileiq.com shows the copyright holder as Bending Spoons US Inc.

Both of those are what the respective pages say. We draw no conclusion from the difference.

What we couldn’t determine

  • Whether anything other than the drive cap separates the two plans, for the reason given above
  • Whether MileIQ’s price has changed, and if so when. This page records what it costs today; we have no verified record of previous pricing
  • Whether the in-app subscription price on iOS matches the price on MileIQ’s website, which App Store listings do not show
  • Whether the 40-drive cap counts only automatically detected drives or manually added ones too, which the page does not specify
