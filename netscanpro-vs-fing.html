NetScanPro vs. Fing

NetScanPro and Fing are both free to download and both cap their free tier at three scans a day. What separates them is what happens next: NetScanPro sells a single paid unlock, Fing sells subscriptions. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, Fing’s pricing page and Fing’s privacy documentation — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such. NetScanPro is published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which also operates AppMatchup; see the disclosure below.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-08 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

NetScanPro

An on-device iPhone scanner from a solo developer, free to scan with a single paid unlock for port scanning and security summaries.

Published by Fogarty Holdings LLC, which operates AppMatchup.

Fing

A long-established scanner sold as a free tier plus subscription plans, with an account, desktop and web clients, and an optional hardware add-on.

If you scan occasionally and want it to stay on your phone with no account, NetScanPro is the pay-once option. If you want the network watched continuously, with alerts when something new joins, Fing is built for that, and that is what the subscription buys.

Where each one fits

NetScanPro is a single $6.99 unlock rather than a subscription, it has no accounts and no sign-in, and its one outbound request goes to a device that answered on the user’s own network, so nothing leaves the local network. Each scanned port carries a plain-language explanation, which is aimed at people who don’t already know what a port does. Three things sit against that. It runs on iPhone and iPad only. It offers no continuous monitoring or alerts. And it is far newer and far less widely used than Fing — the rating counts in the table above are the clearest measure of that gap.

Fing has the widest reach of the two — Windows and Mac desktop apps, a Raspberry Pi agent, a web app and an Android version, plus Fingbox as an optional hardware add-on — and it is the only one of the two that lists continuous monitoring and alerts, on paid plans. Against that, its free plan allows up to 3 manual scans per day, the same cap as NetScanPro’s. Every plan, including the free one, sits behind a sign-up. And its monitoring requires Fing Desktop, Fing Agent or Fingbox running separately, on top of the subscription.

What we couldn’t verify

These are questions the published sources do not answer. They are listed here unresolved rather than guessed at.

  • Whether Fing’s free plan can be used without creating an account
  • What Fing’s device recognition patent covers, which its App Store description asserts without detail
  • How many devices on a typical network broadcast a MAC address NetScanPro can read; iOS restricts access to the address table, so this depends entirely on the mix of devices present
  • What Fing’s paid open-ports finder adds over the free one, beyond the pricing page labelling them basic and advanced
  • How either app’s identification performs in practice, which no published source establishes
