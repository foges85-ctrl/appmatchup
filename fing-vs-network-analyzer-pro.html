Fing vs. Network Analyzer Pro

Fing is published by Fing Limited and has been on the App Store since 2011-04-19. Network Analyzer Pro is published by Jiri Techet and has been available since 2012-09-18. Fing is free to download with paid subscription tiers; Network Analyzer Pro is a single paid purchase. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings, Fing’s pricing page and both developers’ privacy policies — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-08 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

Fing

A network scanner sold as a free tier plus subscription plans, with an account, desktop and web clients, and an optional hardware add-on.

Network Analyzer Pro

A single-purchase diagnostics toolkit from an independent developer, with no account and no subscription.

If you want continuous monitoring and alerts and don’t mind an account and a recurring bill, Fing is built for that. If you scan occasionally and would rather pay once, Network Analyzer Pro covers the same ground on the phone and costs less than a month of Fing’s cheaper plan.

Where each one fits

Fing is the only one of the two that lists continuous monitoring and alerts, and it comes with more around it — Windows and Mac desktop apps, a Raspberry Pi agent, a web app, and Fingbox as an optional hardware add-on. Three things sit against that. Its monitoring requires Fing Desktop, Fing Agent or Fingbox running separately, so the phone app on its own does not provide it. Its free plan allows up to 3 manual scans per day, with unlimited scans on paid plans at $7.99/month or $69.99/year for Premium and $16.99/month or $149.99/year for Professional. And its pricing page presents every plan, including the free one, behind a sign-up.

Network Analyzer Pro is $3.99 once, with no account and no subscription, and it lists the broader set of on-demand diagnostic tools of the two — whois and DNS lookup including DNSSEC record types alongside ping, traceroute, port scanning and a speed test. It is a far smaller install than Fing, as the table above shows. Against that, it does not list continuous monitoring or alerts, and it requires iOS 18.0 where Fing runs on 16.4.
