MileIQ vs. Stride

MileIQ is published by MileIQ Inc. and has been on the App Store since 2013-01-15. Stride is published by Stride Health and has been available since 2015-10-12. Both track mileage automatically by GPS, and both are free to download — but they make money in completely different ways. This comparison is built from what each developer publishes — App Store listings and MileIQ’s own pricing page — with every claim sourced and anything unverifiable listed as such.

Everything below comes from published sources, checked on 2026-08-10 and linked in each section. Where the sources don’t answer a question, it’s listed under What we couldn’t verify rather than guessed at.

MileIQ

A dedicated automatic mileage tracker sold on a subscription, with a capped free tier and IRS-format reporting.

Stride

A free tax and expense app for self-employed workers, with automatic mileage tracking alongside deduction finding and health insurance enrolment.

MileIQ does one job and charges for it. Stride does more jobs and charges for none of them, because its business is selling health insurance to the self-employed. If mileage is all you need and you drive more than the free tier allows, MileIQ is the focused tool. If you want deductions and tax filing in the same app and don’t mind what it’s really selling, Stride costs nothing.

Where each one fits

MileIQ is a single-purpose tool with thirteen years on the App Store behind it, and the rating count in the table above is the clearest measure of its scale. It lists classification controls Stride does not — swipe right for business, left for personal, with custom categories, notes and purposes — and it has a separate Teams product for tracking employee mileage. Against that, its free tier stops at 40 drives a month, and unlimited tracking costs $11.66 to $13.99 a month depending on how you pay. What MileIQ costs sets out both plans and what the 40-drive cap means in practice.

Stride is free with no stated drive limit, and it is far broader: expenses, deductions, filing and insurance enrolment in one app. Against that, its listing does not say how drives are classified as business or personal, and the free tools sit alongside an insurance marketplace — which is worth understanding before choosing it.

What we couldn’t verify
